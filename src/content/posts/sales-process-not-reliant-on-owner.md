In most commercial coatings companies, the owner is the sales team. That works fine at a certain size — and becomes the ceiling on growth past it. If every bid needs the owner on the call to close, the business can only grow as fast as the owner's calendar allows.

## Why "Just Hire a Salesperson" Doesn't Work Alone

Hiring a salesperson without a documented process usually just moves the bottleneck. A new hire without a defined discovery-call script, objection-handling framework, and follow-up cadence ends up improvising — and improvising rarely closes at the same rate the owner does, because the owner's process was never actually written down in the first place.

## What a Documented Sales Process Actually Includes

- **A discovery-call script** that surfaces budget, timeline, and decision-maker information early, not on the third call.
- **A bid-presentation framework** that walks the buyer through scope, warranty, and value — not just a line-item price sheet.
- **Objection-handling scripts** built for the objections that actually come up in commercial coatings sales, not generic sales-training material.
- **A follow-up cadence** for bids that go quiet, since stalled deals are usually a follow-up-system problem, not a lost cause.

## Start by Documenting What Already Works

The fastest way to build this isn't writing a sales process from scratch — it's documenting what the owner already does instinctively on a call that closes. That instinct is usually a real, teachable process; it's just never been written down in a form someone else can follow.

## Handing It Off Without Losing Close Rate

The goal isn't just documentation — it's a process a new salesperson can run at a close rate that doesn't collapse the moment the owner isn't on the call. That requires role-playing the actual scripts, reviewing real calls, and adjusting the process based on where a new hire actually struggles.
