# Conversion Patterns — coaching/consulting lead-gen (vs. home-service lead-gen)

## Key difference from a repair/install lead-gen site
This is a B2B coaching offer sold to contracting-company owners, not a homeowner-facing repair job. CTAs, form design, and trust signals differ from the tract-home-style build:

- **Primary CTA:** "Book a Free Strategy Call" (not "Get a Quote" / "Call Now"). Phone number is still present for legitimacy but is not the primary conversion lever the way it is on a home-service site.
- **Forms:** A short application-style intake (name, phone, email, company name, approx. annual revenue range or company size, biggest challenge dropdown) is standard for coaching lead-gen — slightly more fields than a 4-field home-service form is acceptable here because it's a considered, higher-ticket B2B decision, not an impulse call.
- **Trust signals:** Methodology and credibility-based, NOT fabricated client results, revenue-growth numbers, or star ratings. Hard rule for this build: no invented "helped X contractor go from $500K to $2M" case studies and no fake testimonials. Defensible trust claims only: CCA's 20+ years of former-contractor experience, trade-specific program design, free/no-obligation strategy call, direct access to the coach (not a call center).
- **Social proof placement:** Since no fabricated testimonials are used, lean on process transparency instead — "what happens on your strategy call," clear program structure, and FAQ depth in place of review counts.
- **Sticky mobile CTA:** Adapt the home-service pattern to "Book a Call" + phone, rather than "Call Now" + "Book Online" (there's no same-day emergency framing in coaching).
